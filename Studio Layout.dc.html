ONE CASE

Cross-Border Invoicing Flow

CONTEXT

We needed to design a cross-border invoicing flow for different countries, taking into account local legal requirements and different steps users need to complete.

CONSTRAINTS
  • Different legal requirements across countries
  • Complex forms and high effort for users
  • Risk of high cognitive load with many questions on one screen
DECISION

I broke the flow into small, logical steps and adapted the questions to each jurisdiction.

The main criteria were task completion and question clarity. We chose shorter forms instead of long screens with many questions to reduce cognitive load.

OUTCOME
  • 95% task completion in pre-launch UX testing (n=15)
  • 44 sec. for the first attempt without training
  • 30 sec. for repeat attempts
  • Updated questions for the jurisdiction where one user had difficulties
  • Step-by-step structure made it easier to track analytics, find bugs, and improve individual steps without changing the whole flow
IMPACT

A scalable flow that can be adapted to new jurisdictions and optimized step by step.
